\subsection{Identification and inference for the anchored test}
\label{sec:anchored-theory}
\paragraph{Setup.} Take source $s$ against reference $r$ in hour $t$, in bps, with $u_{r,t}$ absorbed. Items other than the
anchor share the offset $c_t=u_t+\delta_t$, where $u_t$ is the unit offset and $\delta_t$ is source-wide item demand, which
enters every non-anchor item and not the anchor. The cross-item offset of Eq.~\eqref{eq:median} is $L_t=c_t+\varepsilon_t$.
The anchor $D_t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:anchor}) and its netted form $Y_t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:netted}) are as in \S\ref{sec:model}. The estimand is the unit share of the offset's
time variation (Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda}),
\begin{equation}
\lambda=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(u,c)}{\mathrm{Var}(c)}=1-\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(\delta,c)}{\mathrm{Var}(c)}.
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
$\lambda=1$ under a pure unit shift ($\delta\equiv0$), whenever demand is uncorrelated with the offset, and $\lambda=0$ under pure
demand ($u\equiv0$). The moments leave intercepts free, so a persistent level premium from demand is not identified, and if
demand co-moves with the unit ($\delta=\kappa u$), $\lambda=1/(1+\kappa)$: $\lambda$ is a covariance share, not an additive share
of the premium.
\emph{A1} (additivity): the model above, with unit loading on $c$ for every non-anchor item (not tested asset by asset), and $\varepsilon$ independent of $(u,\delta,\eta)$.
\emph{A2}: the reference venue's unit is stable over the window ($u_r$ is constant).
\emph{A3}: $X$ measures $x$ on a venue whose error $\xi$ is independent of $(u,\delta,\varepsilon,\eta)$; $x$ may co-move with $c$ (we write $\eta$ for $\eta-\xi$ below).
\emph{A4}: a second item's offset $Z_t=\gamma c_t+\nu_t$, $\gamma\neq0$, with $\nu$ independent of $(u,\delta,\eta,\varepsilon)$ and
$\mathrm{Cov}(\eta,c)=\mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon,\eta)=0$ (relevance and exclusion); these are assumptions we cannot test.

\begin{proposition}
Under A1--A4, the instrumental-variable (IV) ratio identifies the unit share,
\begin{equation}
\beta=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(D-X,\,Z)}{\mathrm{Cov}(L,\,Z)}=\lambda .
\label{eq:iv}
\end{equation}
The OLS slope of $D-X$ on $L$ is $\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}(c)/(\mathrm{Var}(c)+\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon))$, attenuated by the noise in $L$.
Without the netting, the IV slope of $D$ on $L$ is
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\mathrm{raw}}=\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(x,c)}{\mathrm{Var}(c)} .
\label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{Cov}(Y,Z)=\gamma\,\mathrm{Cov}(u+\eta,c)=\gamma\,\mathrm{Cov}(u,c)$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(L,Z)=\gamma\,\mathrm{Var}(c)$ by A4; the ratio is $\lambda$. The
OLS slope is $\mathrm{Cov}(u,c)/\mathrm{Var}(L)$ with $\mathrm{Var}(L)=\mathrm{Var}(c)+\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon)$. Without $X$, $\mathrm{Cov}(D,Z)$ gains $\gamma\,\mathrm{Cov}(x,c)$.
\end{proof}
\emph{Intuition:} $Z$ moves with the offset but not with the anchor's own noise, so the ratio isolates how much of the
anchor's co-movement with the offset runs through $u$. The bias term $\mathrm{Cov}(x,c)/\mathrm{Var}(c)$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias} is the token's own co-movement with the offset; $D-X$ contains $u$ but, up to the independent
error $\xi$, not $x$, so that term drops out of Eq.~\eqref{eq:iv}.

\paragraph{Reading $\hat\lambda$.} On the Bitfinex stress window $\hat\lambda=\NumGBfxPrimaryLam{}$. In words: when the
offset moved, the netted anchor moved almost one for one, so most of the offset's time variation was a unit shift and
the remainder was demand or an anchor-specific component. It does not say that the offset's \emph{level} on any date
was that share unit and the rest demand (intercepts are free), it does not split each hour, and, because $\lambda=1$ is
rejected, it does not say the shift was purely unit.

\paragraph{Over-identification.} With anchors $k=1,\dots,K$ and instruments $j=1,\dots,m$, take $z=(1,Z_1,\dots,Z_m)$ and the moments
\begin{equation}
E\big[z_j\,(Y_k-\mu_k-\lambda L)\big]=0,\qquad j=0,\dots,m,\ k=1,\dots,K,
\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
which (Eq.~\eqref{eq:moments}) are $K(m+1)$ conditions for $K+1$ parameters ($\lambda$ and the intercepts $\mu_k$). Two-step GMM with a Bartlett heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) weight $\hat S^{-1}$ gives Hansen's statistic (Eq.~\eqref{eq:J})
\begin{equation}
J=n\,\bar g(\hat\lambda)'\,\hat S^{-1}\,\bar g(\hat\lambda)\ \sim\ \chi^2_{Km-1},
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
with $\bar g$ the sample moments. For the headline, $K=1$ and $m=4$, so $J$ has \NumGBfxPrimaryJDf{} degrees of freedom. \emph{Intuition:} if one $\lambda$ explains every
moment, the leftover moments are small; $J$ measures how large they are. $J$ rejects a contaminated instrument or anchor-specific depegs with different
loadings on $c$; it has no power against a depeg that loads on $c$ equally in every anchor, so the netting by $X$ is what
removes that bias, not the test.

\paragraph{Inference.} Hourly offsets are persistent ($\hat\rho_c=\NumGCalRhoC{}$ on the stress window), so we use HAC standard errors
with Andrews' bandwidth (the fixed Newey--West rule is a check) and a moving-block bootstrap with block length
$b=\lceil(2\rho^2/(1-\rho^2)^2)^{1/3}n^{1/3}\rceil$, $\rho$ the lag-one autocorrelation of the IV score.

\paragraph{Evidence.} Table~\ref{tab:theory} gives \NumGSimReps{} simulated windows of \NumGSimHours{} hours per scenario,
calibrated to the stress window. IV is unbiased in a pure shift (bias \NumGSOneBiasIv{}), a mixture (\NumGSThreeBiasIv{}), a netted depeg
(\NumGSFiveBiasIv{}) and, with noise in $L$ as large as its signal, \NumGSSixBiasIv{}, where OLS is biased by
\NumGSSixBiasOls{}. Leaving the depeg in gives a bias of \NumGSFourBiasIv{}, of the order of the data's gap between \NumGBfxNaiveLam{} (hourly GMM; the daily OLS slope is \NumKStressDaySlopeD{}) and \NumGBfxPrimaryLam{}.
HAC intervals cover at \NumGSOneCover{} in a pure shift but at \NumGSThreeCover{} in the mixture, where the realised unit
share varies by window; the block bootstrap is no better there (\NumGSThreeCoverBoot{}). $J$ has size \NumGSOneJRej{} but
rejects at only \NumGSFourJRej{} when every anchor's depeg loads alike and at \NumGSSevenJRej{} when loadings differ.
On the Bitfinex stress window ($K=1$, $m=4$, \NumGBfxPrimaryN{} hours, \NumGBfxPrimaryJDf{} df), $\hat\lambda=\NumGBfxPrimaryLam{}$ with $J=\NumGBfxPrimaryJ{}$
($p=\NumGBfxPrimaryJP{}$); $\lambda=0$ is rejected ($p$ \NumGBfxPrimaryPZero{}), and so is $\lambda=1$ ($p$ \NumGBfxPrimaryPOne{}).
Intervals for this one estimate: hourly GMM, HAC \NumGBfxPrimaryLo{} to \NumGBfxPrimaryHi{}; hourly moving-block bootstrap
(block \NumGBfxBootBlock{} hours), \NumGBfxBootLo{} to \NumGBfxBootHi{}; daily OLS slope of \S\ref{sec:anchor}, week blocks
\NumKStressDaySlopeAdjWkLo{} to \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjWkHi{} and month blocks \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjMoLo{} to
\NumKStressDaySlopeAdjMoHi{}. The hourly intervals are narrower than the daily ones, not wider; because HAC and the hourly
bootstrap undercover when the offset is persistent, the headline elsewhere is the month-block interval. Without the netting the estimate is \NumGBfxNaiveLam{}.
Binance.US 2023 ($K=1$, $m=2$, \NumGBusUsdtN{} hours) gives \NumGBusUsdtLam{} (HAC \NumGBusUsdtLo{} to \NumGBusUsdtHi{};
$p=\NumGBusUsdtPOne{}$ for $\lambda=1$), a HAC interval that is probably too narrow, and the netting changes nothing there
(two anchors, February to July: \NumGBusTwoLam{} netted, \NumGBusTwoRawLam{} not). Adding USDC netted with Bitstamp's
depegged USDC price fails the over-identification test ($J=\NumGBusTwoJ{}$, $p$ \NumGBusTwoJP{}; not netted, $J=\NumGBusTwoRawJ{}$,
$p=\NumGBusTwoRawJP{}$). We report every $J$ and did not fix in advance which results count.
