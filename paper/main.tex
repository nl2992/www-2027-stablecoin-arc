\title{Dislocated Dollars, Aligned Quotes: A Pre-Registered Multi-Event Study of Stablecoin Fragmentation}

\begin{abstract}
Stablecoins are meant to trade at par across venues and quote currencies, yet stress episodes repeatedly produce
prices that differ across markets for hours or days. We present a descriptive, pre-registered multi-event study of
how such fragmentation arises and resolves. A sourced registry of \NumRegistryEntries{} candidate episodes from
2020--2026, explicit severity, independence and coverage rules, matched control windows, placebo and
expected-negative cases, a rule-based screen of venue data, and a post-2024 out-of-sample set yield
\NumAcceptedClusters{} accepted independent episodes: the TerraUSD collapse (2022) and the USDC/SVB run (2023),
both exploratory, and the TUSD (2024) and FDUSD (2025) dislocations, both out-of-sample. Across them we separate a
stablecoin's own dollar price from the within-venue cross-quote wedge. The dollar price dislocated by hundreds to
thousands of basis points while the wedge barely moved (median change \NumAnchorDvFour{}, \NumTusdDvFour{} and
\NumFdusdDvFour{} bps for USDC, TUSD and FDUSD): cross-quote arbitrage kept relative prices aligned while the dollar
value of the token did not hold. The episodes also split into market-wide and issuer-specific stress. Because no
accepted episode is both confirmatory and in-sample, the pre-registered hypothesis tests are reported as
insufficient, and all findings are descriptive. Executable restoration capacity is formalised and validated; on-chain
absorption capacity, measured on swap-validated Curve pools, contracted in every stress episode examined, but exchange
depth could not be measured, so no CEX--DEX capacity is reported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Fiat-backed and synthetic stablecoins rely on arbitrage to hold the peg: when the secondary-market price
falls below par, traders buy and redeem; when relative prices across quote currencies diverge, traders route
across them. Both mechanisms need \emph{capacity}---the quantity that can actually be executed at a given cost
within a given time. Prior work shows that arbitrage design matters for peg stability~\cite{lyons2023keeps},
that primary-market access shapes crisis dynamics beyond what secondary prices reveal~\cite{watsky2024primary,du2025shadow},
and that cross-exchange crypto price deviations can be large and persistent when arbitrage capital is
constrained~\cite{makarov2020trading}.

\paragraph{Why multiple events.} A single crisis cannot separate mechanisms: in March 2023, bank-reserve risk,
weekend redemption closure and exchange-specific liquidity all moved together. We therefore pre-specify a
multi-event design covering algorithmic collapses, bank-reserve runs, exchange-failure contagion, issuer
solvency allegations and venue-infrastructure failures, with negative cases and placebo shocks evaluated
under the same rules. This improves on a March 2023-only study in three ways: (i) heterogeneity across
mechanisms becomes testable rather than assumed away; (ii) selection is rule-based and includes events
expected \emph{not} to qualify; (iii) post-2024 events are held out, so conclusions drawn from the in-sample
period face an honest test.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A pre-registered, machine-validated multi-event design: a sourced event registry with independence clustering,
severity and coverage rules, placebo and negative cases, a rule-based screen, an out-of-sample set, and a frozen
analysis plan with a public amendment log (\S\ref{sec:events}). (2) A measurement framework that separates peg
dislocation, within-venue triangle wedges and cross-venue wedges, and distinguishes parity deviations from gross,
executable and realised arbitrage (\S\ref{sec:framework}). (3) Descriptive cross-event evidence that the dollar price
and the cross-quote wedge behave very differently in stress, and that episodes divide into market-wide and
issuer-specific types (\S\ref{sec:multievent}). (4) Reproducible, offline-validated methods for executable
restoration capacity on order books and AMM pools, with the data limits that currently prevent historical
estimation stated explicitly.

\paragraph{Scope.} The study is descriptive. It does not identify causal effects, and it does not claim that the
findings generalise beyond the accepted episodes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Limits to arbitrage.} Classic theory explains why mispricing can persist when arbitrage is
performed by specialized, capital-constrained agents~\cite{shleifer1997limits}; the survey
of~\cite{gromb2010limits} organizes these frictions into fundamental and non-fundamental risk, short-selling
costs, leverage and margin constraints, and equity-capital constraints. Our restoration-capacity measure is an
execution-level counterpart: it asks how much quantity could actually be routed at a given cost, rather than
assuming that any visible price gap is arbitraged.

\paragraph{Crypto market segmentation.} Large, recurrent cross-exchange price deviations, with greater
segmentation across than within countries, are documented by~\cite{makarov2020trading}. We study the
stablecoin analogue, where the deviation is between quote currencies and between a token and its peg.

\paragraph{Stablecoin pegs, redemption and runs.} Improved access to primary-market arbitrage roughly halved
Tether's peg deviations in~\cite{lyons2023keeps}. \cite{ma2025runs} show that major issuers concentrate
redemption in a few authorized arbitrageurs, trading off secondary-market price stability against run risk.
\cite{anadu2023runs} document flight-to-safety flows across stablecoins in stress and a break-the-buck threshold
near \$1. Federal Reserve staff analyses stress that secondary prices alone do not reveal primary-market
conditions~\cite{watsky2024primary} and describe USDC's loss of peg when primary operations paused over the SVB
weekend~\cite{du2025shadow}. The legal framing of stablecoin issuers as unregulated banks is developed
by~\cite{zhang2023taming}. Relative to this literature we add (i) a pre-registered, multi-event, multi-mechanism
event design and (ii) a separation of peg dislocation from cross-quote wedges that maps directly to the
redemption versus secondary-market channels.

\paragraph{AMM liquidity.} StableSwap~\cite{egorov2019stableswap} and concentrated
liquidity~\cite{adams2021uniswapv3} determine on-chain execution costs for stablecoin swaps; \cite{lehar2025uniswap}
compare Uniswap with a centralized limit order book and document the absence of long-lived arbitrage
opportunities between them. Our on-chain capacity measures will use historically pinned pool states rather than
current protocol parameters.

\section{Measurement Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
For stablecoin $q$ with USD peg, let $S_t^{q}$ be its USD price on a venue. We distinguish:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Peg dislocation} $D_t^{q} = 10^4 \log S_t^{q}$ (bps).
\item \textbf{Within-venue triangle wedge} $W_t^{q} = 10^4[\log P_t(\mathrm{BTC}/q) + \log S_t^{q} - \log P_t(\mathrm{BTC}/\mathrm{USD})]$,
which is zero under frictionless cross-quote arbitrage on one venue regardless of $S_t^q$.
\item \textbf{Cross-venue wedge}: the same construction with legs from different venues.
\end{itemize}
A nonzero $D$ or $W$ is a \emph{parity deviation}. It is a \emph{gross opportunity} only after quoted
spreads, a \emph{executable opportunity} only after fees, depth, latency, settlement and inventory, and a
\emph{realized trade} only when observed in trade data. Restoration capacity $K_t(\delta)$ is the
largest quantity that can be executed around the loop at all-in cost at most $\delta$ within horizon $h$.
Candle data identify parity deviations only; $K_t$ requires order-book or on-chain execution state.

\section{Pre-Registered Questions}
The frozen plan names three hypotheses, retained here as questions. \textbf{H1}: are fragmentation magnitude and
persistence higher in stress windows than in matched controls? \textbf{H2}: are peg dislocations more persistent
than within-venue wedges when redemption access is impaired? \textbf{H3}: conditional on stress, is lower executable
restoration capacity associated with larger and more persistent wedges? The plan's confirmatory test for H1 requires
accepted in-sample episodes measured after the plan was frozen; none exists, so H1 is reported as insufficient and
the evidence is presented descriptively. H2's ratio statistic proves unstable and exceeds one even in the placebo
(\S\ref{sec:multievent}). H3 requires historical execution state that is not available. Decision rules, dependent
variables, multiplicity control and the out-of-sample protocol are fixed in a pre-analysis plan frozen by content
hash before any non-anchor event was measured; amendments are logged with whether data had been seen.

\section{Event Selection}
\label{sec:events}
Rules were fixed before measuring any non-anchor event (Table~\ref{tab:registry}). \emph{R1 (severity):} at least 30 traded minutes with
$|D| \ge 100$ bps inside some 24-hour window; $\ge 500$ bps marks the severe class. \emph{R2 (independence):} one
episode per primary trigger; contagion legs share their primary's cluster and never add episodes.
\emph{R3 (windows):} 7-day baseline ending two days before the event, the event window, a 7-day recovery, and a
7-day matched control ending 28 days before. \emph{R4 (sourcing):} at least two distinct publishers.
Events from 2024 onward are out-of-sample.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Anchor.} A 30,240-minute Binance.US one-minute candle panel (2023-03-01 to 2023-03-21), hash-pinned and
audited, provides USDC/USD, USDT/USD and the BTC legs of both triangles on a single venue.

\paragraph{Multi-event candle sources.} For the remaining candidates, the only free public archives identified with
complete windows are Binance.com monthly one-minute klines. They are listed (with byte sizes and ETags) for UST,
TUSD, FDUSD, USDe and the BUSD placebo, together with the BTC/stablecoin legs needed for within-venue triangles,
except that no BTC/USDe market is listed (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}, Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}). None has yet been
downloaded. Archives are accepted only after checksum verification; timestamp units (milliseconds before 2025,
microseconds after) are inferred per file, and missing minutes are kept missing rather than filled.

\paragraph{Quote basis.} Binance.com quotes these stablecoins in USDT, so a raw series measures deviation from USDT,
not from the dollar. Following the pre-analysis plan, such series enter confirmatory tests only after conversion with
a USD reference leg on a USD venue and only in windows where USDT/USD itself stays within 10~bps of par. The
designated reference is Binance.US USDT/USD history; Bitstamp's public USDT/USD market is a robustness alternative.
Without the reference leg an event can be measured but not accepted. The within-venue triangle
$W=\log P(\mathrm{BTC}/q)+\log P(q/\mathrm{USDT})-\log P(\mathrm{BTC}/\mathrm{USDT})$ is unaffected, because USDT
cancels.

\paragraph{Order books and on-chain state.} Order-book reconstruction is validated on one public normalized L2 sample
(\NumSampleRows{} rows; BTCUSD, 2023-03-01)~\cite{tardis2026binanceus}; normalized feeds omit native exchange
sequence identifiers~\cite{coinapi2026limitbook}, so continuity is disclosed as unverifiable. Event-specific Curve
pools are source-documented (for May 2022, the wormhole-UST/3CRV pool analysed by~\cite{jump2022ust}). Using a free
archive endpoint, the state of Curve's 3pool (March and June 2023) and of both UST metapools (May 2022) was read at the
block preceding each event window, and every on-chain \texttt{get\_dy} quote was reproduced exactly by our offline
StableSwap implementation; no capacity has yet been computed from these states. Candle closes are last trades, not quotes.

\paragraph{UST and post-hoc eligibility.} Binance removed UST/USDT on 2022-05-13 and UST continued only against BUSD
until the token was renamed USTC (USTC/BUSD from 2022-05-30). A pre-registered splice onto USTC/BUSD (A4) left the

UST then qualifies, with an extreme of \NumUstExtreme{} bps and no recovery (Table~\ref{tab:results}), but because its
event-window result was known before A5, it is treated as exploratory and excluded from confirmatory counts.

\paragraph{A caution on H2.} The peg-to-wedge half-life ratio exceeds one in every event where it is defined,
\emph{including the placebo} (\NumPlaceboDvFive), and it is numerically unstable when the wedge half-life is near zero
(Table~\ref{tab:results}). A ratio above one is therefore not by itself evidence of stress-specific persistence;
persistence comparisons must be made against each event's own control.

\paragraph{Robustness.} Every event's R1 classification is unchanged at 50, 100 and 200~bps, and the trimmed-window
DV1 keeps its sign (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}). Half-lives are fragile: undefined for UST, which never mean-reverts,
near a unit root for TUSD, and increasing with bin width for FDUSD, so persistence is reported descriptively.

\section{Executable Capacity: Method and Limits}
We formalise two quantities that the framework keeps separate. \emph{Standardised restoration capacity}
$K_t(\delta)$ is the largest quantity executable at all-in cost at most $\delta$ relative to a reference price, solved
exactly level by level on an order book. \emph{Restoration quantity} is the size routed around a triangle loop until
its marginal unit no longer pays after fees, in either direction, with depth and inventory limits reported as binding
constraints. On-chain legs use integer StableSwap, Uniswap~v2 and single-range Uniswap~v3 swap arithmetic, and
primary-market access is modelled by a timezone-aware redemption window. All components are validated against
hand-computed synthetic cases.

\paragraph{On-chain results (descriptive; on-chain only).} For pools whose reproduced state was also validated
against a real historical swap, we sample state every six hours across each event's windows
(Table~\ref{tab:onchain}). Absorption capacity fell in all three cases. It reached zero where the pool filled with the
stressed coin (UST in May 2022; the USDC-to-USDT leg in March 2023), while restoration capacity---the stressed coin
available to buyers---rose. In June 2023, 3pool absorption fell to about a third of its baseline as the pool
became USDT-heavy, yet USDT's dollar price on a venue with redeemable dollars never met the severity rule: on-chain
contraction alone did not produce an exchange-level dislocation. With three pools these are associations, and they
measure on-chain capacity only.

Historical estimation of exchange capacity is not possible with the data available to this study. Crisis-day
exchange order books are offered only under paid licences, which were declined. Historical pool state is readable through a free archive key; pool states
and quotes were reproduced exactly at event blocks and validated against real swaps. Crisis-day exchange depth remains
unavailable, so the capacity reported above is on-chain only and does not represent comprehensive CEX--DEX
restoration capacity. No exchange capacity figures are reported.

\section{Limitations}
Four accepted episodes (two exploratory, none confirmatory in-sample); one amendment made after seeing data; last-trade candles with asynchronous legs and bid-ask bounce; calendar regime labels;
USDT-quoted archives require USD correction; no executable depth; normalized L2 lacks native sequence IDs.
