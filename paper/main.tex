\title{Which Dollar Broke? Separating Token Failures from Platform-Ledger Failures in Web Dollar Markets}

\begin{abstract}
Crypto prices on the Web are quoted in two kinds of dollar: stablecoin tokens, and balances in an exchange's internal
ledger that reach the banking system only through the platform's rails. Price feeds and oracles average these quotes
as if every USD quote were in the same dollar, and we know of no measure that tells a token failure from a ledger failure.
This is hard because a ledger's value is never quoted directly, public prices are asynchronous last trades that thin
out under stress, and any single asset may carry its own shock. We price the platform's dollar through unpegged assets
against venues whose rails stay open, which splits a dollar-quoted dislocation into a token and a ledger component, and
we accept the split only when unrelated assets agree and the venue's internal cross-rates hold. We study rail-cut
episodes on three platforms: three Bitfinex windows from 2017 to 2019, Binance.US in 2023, and Binance's sterling and
euro rails. On Bitfinex and Binance.US the platform's dollar, not a token, moved: by up to \NumPanSeventeenMax{} bps on
Bitfinex, with BTC and ETH agreeing to within \NumPanGapMax{} bps and internal cross-rates holding to within
\NumPanTriMax{} bps. On both platforms it traded at a discount while exit was impaired and later at a premium. A
volume-weighted BTC/USD index over these venues was off by at least 100 bps for \NumFeedNaiveHours{} hours; dropping the
venue-hours our rule flags removes \NumFeedRemovedShare\% of them. On Bitfinex in 2018 the discount appeared
\NumFeedLeadDays{} days before the deposit freeze was first reported and about \NumFeedLeadMonths{} months before
regulators disclosed the loss behind it. In the March 2023 USDC run, by contrast, the method attributes all but
\NumDecSvbShare\% of the dislocation to the token. The sterling and euro cuts show only small effects. All findings are
descriptive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Web has acquired its own dollars. A stablecoin is a bearer token on a public blockchain that promises one US
dollar; an exchange balance is an entry in a platform's private ledger that promises the same thing, redeemable only
through that platform's banking partners. Price feeds, wallets and on-chain oracles quote almost every crypto asset in
one of these two dollars. When a price ``in dollars'' moves away from par, users, platforms and regulators need to know
\emph{which dollar broke}: did the token lose value against bank money, or did the platform's ledger lose its link to
bank money? On one venue, Binance.US, both happened within four months. During the March 2023 run on USDC, its USDC/USD
price fell by more than a thousand basis points; in June 2023, after the exchange lost its banking
partners~\cite{theblock2023binanceus}, its USDT/USD price moved by several hundred basis points, and by
\NumPdUsdtWorstDayAbs{} bps on its worst day. To a reader of either price series, both look like stablecoin depegs.
Figure~\ref{fig:which} shows that they are not.

Existing work cannot separate the two. Studies of stablecoin pegs and runs treat a token's exchange price as the
token's value~\cite{lyons2023keeps,ma2025runs,liu2023anatomy}, which is correct only if the exchange's dollar is
itself worth a dollar. Studies of cross-exchange price gaps~\cite{makarov2020trading,choi2019kimchi} measure how far
one asset's price differs between venues, but not whether the asset or the venue's unit of account moved. Reports
of trapped platform money, such as the Mt.~Gox premium of 2013--14~\cite{financemagnates2014mtgox}, describe the
phenomenon without a test that attributes it to the ledger.

Our key idea is that a platform's dollar can be priced like any other asset. If every asset on a venue becomes
dearer or cheaper by the same amount relative to venues whose rails are open, the venue's unit has moved; if only one
token moves, and it moves everywhere, the token has failed. An asset with no peg, such as BTC, makes this measurable
from public data alone.

Three properties of the problem make this harder than it sounds. First, a ledger's value is never quoted directly:
it is visible only through prices denominated in it. Second, public price data are last trades on one-minute
candles, asynchronous across venues and thinnest exactly when stress is greatest. Third, a single asset can have its
own shock, so a gap in one price cannot be attributed to the unit, and crises worth studying are usually chosen
after the fact, which invites selection.

We address each in turn. We price the ledger through an unpegged asset against a reference venue whose rails stayed
open, which gives a token component and a ledger component for any dollar-quoted dislocation (\S\ref{sec:framework}).
We use only minutes traded on every leg, report hourly and phase medians with hour-block bootstrap intervals, and
report liquidity alongside prices. We require unrelated assets to agree, the venue's internal cross-rates to hold, and
the result to survive a second reference. For selection, the token side follows a pre-registered event protocol, and
the ledger side discloses the search that produced it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A \emph{decomposition} of any dollar-quoted dislocation into token and platform-ledger components from public Web
data, with identification, consistency and coherence checks (\S\ref{sec:framework}).
(2) A documented \emph{natural experiment} in platform money: when Binance.US lost its banks in 2023, its dollar's
price followed which rail was open, carried the same dislocation in three unrelated assets, survived liquidity,
reference and boundary checks, and reverted on restoration (\S\ref{sec:ledger}). The method needs only an unpegged
asset and an open-rail reference; the magnitude is one platform's, and two rail cuts on Binance.com show only small effects.
(3) An hourly \emph{monitor} that price feeds and oracles could run, evaluated over every month with overlapping data
and across threshold settings (\S\ref{sec:monitor}), and an on-chain view showing that decentralized pools register
token flows but not ledger failures (\S\ref{sec:onchain}).
(4) A \emph{pre-registered multi-event study} of the token side and an open pipeline in which every input is
checksum- or hash-pinned and every number is generated from committed reports (\S\ref{sec:token}).

\paragraph{Scope.} The study is descriptive. It identifies which layer failed in the measured episodes; it does not
estimate causal effects or claim that the ledger-side magnitude generalises to other platforms.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Limits to arbitrage and segmentation.} Mispricing persists when arbitrage is costly or
capital-constrained~\cite{shleifer1997limits,gromb2010limits}. Crypto prices differ persistently across exchanges,
more across than within countries~\cite{makarov2020trading}; capital controls sustained a Korean premium of several
percent~\cite{choi2019kimchi}. Non-atomic arbitrage links centralized and decentralized venues at scale and is
concentrated in few agents~\cite{heimbach2024nonatomic}. These works study price gaps for one asset across venues; we
attribute a gap to the token or to the venue's unit of account.

\paragraph{Stablecoin pegs and runs.} Primary-market access roughly halved Tether's deviations~\cite{lyons2023keeps};
issuers concentrate redemption in a few authorized arbitrageurs~\cite{ma2025runs}; flight-to-safety flows cross
stablecoins in stress~\cite{anadu2023runs}; secondary prices alone do not show primary-market
conditions~\cite{watsky2024primary}, as the SVB weekend showed~\cite{du2025shadow}. The Terra run was led by large,
sophisticated holders~\cite{liu2023anatomy}; issuer design is also a legal question~\cite{zhang2023taming}.
On-chain pool metrics can flag depegs early~\cite{cintra2023detecting}. All of this is the token side.

\paragraph{Platform money.} When Mt.~Gox delayed fiat withdrawals in 2013--14, BTC traded at a large premium there
as users bought crypto to leave~\cite{financemagnates2014mtgox}; this ``escape buying'' is widely reported but, to
our knowledge, has not been measured with an identification check, nor has the reverse case of a ledger premium.

\section{Decomposition Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
Let $P_v(X/F_v)$ be the price of asset $X$ on venue $v$ in that venue's ledger units of fiat $F$, and $P_r(X/F)$ its
price on a reference venue $r$ whose rails are open. Write $\log P_v(X/F_v)=x - \ell_v$, where $x$ is the asset's
value in bank money and $\ell_v$ the value of the venue's fiat ledger relative to bank money (log units). For a
stablecoin $q$ with peg $F$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Observed dislocation} $D_q = 10^4\log P_v(q/F_v)$ (bps).
\item \textbf{Ledger component} $L = 10^4[\log P_v(\mathrm{BTC}/F_v)-\log P_r(\mathrm{BTC}/F)] = -10^4\,\ell_v$, the
platform-dollar dislocation of an asset with no peg. More generally $PD_X$ denotes this quantity for asset $X$.
\item \textbf{Token component} $T_q = D_q - L$, the token's price in external fiat implied through BTC.
\end{itemize}
Three checks make the attribution testable. \emph{Identification:} if the ledger has failed, $PD_X$ is the same for
unrelated assets ($PD_{\mathrm{USDT}}\approx PD_{\mathrm{BTC}}$). \emph{Consistency:} $T_q$ must match the token's
direct price on the reference venue, $D_q^{r}$. \emph{Internal coherence:} the within-venue triangle
$W=\log P_v(\mathrm{BTC}/q)+\log P_v(q/F_v)-\log P_v(\mathrm{BTC}/F_v)$ must stay near zero, so that the venue's
internal prices are not themselves the source. A token failure shows $|T_q|\gg|L|$; a ledger failure shows
$|L|\gg|T_q|$ with $PD$ equal across assets. Candle closes are last trades, so all statistics use minutes traded on
every leg involved and report hourly or phase medians.

\section{Data and Protocol}
\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Sources.} One-minute klines from the public Binance and Binance.US archives (checksum-verified on
download) and one-minute OHLC from Bitstamp's public API (hash-recorded at acquisition); all acquisitions are listed
in a committed manifest. No paid data, accounts or credentials are used. Bitstamp is the reference venue: its USD,
GBP and EUR rails stayed open throughout the windows studied. Binance.US BTC/USD did not trade from mid-July 2023 until
2025, so phase statistics needing BTC cover only the hours in which it traded.

\paragraph{Token side: pre-registration.} Event rules, windows, dependent variables and decision rules were fixed in
a pre-analysis plan frozen by content hash before any non-anchor event was measured; amendments are logged with
whether data had been seen (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). \emph{R1:} at least 30 traded minutes with $|D|\ge 100$ bps
in a 24-hour window. \emph{R2:} one episode per trigger. \emph{R3:} baseline, event, recovery and a matched control
window. \emph{R4:} at least two independent sources. Events from 2024 onward are out-of-sample.

\paragraph{Ledger side: a disclosed search.} The ledger analysis was not pre-registered. After the token-side study
ended without a confirmatory in-sample episode, we searched \NumDirectionsTried{} candidate directions under fixed
kill criteria (visible raw signal, novelty, Web relevance, free authorized data, a replication case); \NumDirectionsDiscarded{}
were discarded, including a calendar effect of bank hours on calm-period deviations, which did not hold. The
Binance.US phase boundaries are documented dates fixed before phase statistics were computed (daily medians had been
inspected). Predictions for the two replication cases were recorded before their data were downloaded; the
recorded rule tested levels rather than changes, a flaw we report rather than repair.

banking partners would stop USD payments from 13 June~\cite{theblock2023binanceus}; on 22 June it delisted most USD
pairs and told users to convert dollars; USD rails were restored on 19 February 2025~\cite{businesswire2025binanceus}.

\paragraph{The ledger, not the tokens, moved.} Before the complaint, Binance.US prices sat a few basis points above

assets became dearer in it, by about \NumPdUsdtHalt{} bps (USDT) and \NumPdBtcHalt{} bps (BTC) in the withdrawal-halt

worst day the median USDT price was \NumPdUsdtWorstDayAbs{} bps below Bitstamp's.

\paragraph{Three assets, one unit.} In every phase except the four days of the deposit suspension, the
hourly correlation between $PD_{\mathrm{USDT}}$ and $PD_{\mathrm{BTC}}$ is at least \NumIdentCorrMin{}
(\NumIdentCorrDep{} during the suspension): a stablecoin and a volatile asset with nothing in common but the unit
they were priced in carried the same dislocation. A third asset, USDC, which traded against the platform dollar

\paragraph{Not a thin-market or reference artifact.} Two checks address the obvious alternatives. \emph{Liquidity:}
with deposits shut, USDT/USD still traded in \NumLiqTransShare\% of minutes, with a median \NumLiqTransTrades{} trades
and \$\NumLiqTransVolK{}k of volume per hour (before the complaint: \NumLiqPreShare\%, \NumLiqPreTrades{} trades,
\$\NumLiqPreVolK{}k); the premium was set in an active, if shrinking, market. \emph{Reference:} valuing BTC through
Binance.com BTC/USDT and Bitstamp USDT/USD instead of Bitstamp BTC/USD gives \NumPdBtcAltHalt{} bps in the
withdrawal-halt window and \NumPdBtcAltTrans{} bps with deposits shut (Table~\ref{tab:phases}, column BTC$^\ast$).

\paragraph{Boundaries and identification.} \emph{Boundaries:} moving every phase boundary by one day either way keeps the deposits-shut median between
\NumSensTransLo{} and \NumSensTransHi{} bps in magnitude and the withdrawal-halt median between \NumSensHaltLo{} and
\NumSensHaltHi{} bps. Because levels that trend together correlate trivially, we also report the correlation of
hourly \emph{changes} in $PD_{\mathrm{USDT}}$ and $PD_{\mathrm{BTC}}$: \NumIdentChgHalt{} in the withdrawal-halt
window and \NumIdentChgTrans{} with deposits shut, where the median USDT--BTC gap is \NumIdentGapTrans\% of the
dislocation's size. During the four days of the deposit suspension the two series agree less well (changes correlation
\NumIdentChgDep), and we do not interpret that phase.

\paragraph{A unit with no anchor.} For the eighteen months in which the platform dollar could
neither enter nor leave, $PD_{\mathrm{USDT}}$ stayed at \NumPdUsdtImpaired{} bps: a closed unit priced only against
USDT has no external anchor, and the small value reflects USDT/USD trading within the venue rather than a valuation.

\paragraph{A one-way gate (hypothesis).} The sign pattern is what a one-way gate predicts. While withdrawals were at
risk, holders sold platform dollars for assets they could carry out (a discount, as at Mt.~Gox). When deposits were
shut but withdrawals still worked, platform dollars became the only route from crypto on the platform to a bank, and
no new dollars could enter to arbitrage the premium. An alternative is delisting mechanics: users told to convert dollars
sold them into a USDT book that had lost depth. Both accounts predict a premium on the platform dollar; the liquidity
evidence above shows the market stayed active, but one platform cannot separate them, and the replication cases below
closed both directions at once.

\paragraph{Replication cases: sterling and euro on Binance.com.} Binance's payment partner stopped sterling transfers
for all users on 22 May 2023~\cite{finder2023gbp} and euro transfers for its users on 25 September
2023~\cite{theblock2023paysafe}. Table~\ref{tab:fiat} applies the same measures against Bitstamp. Both ledgers show
the ledger signature, with USDT and BTC agreeing (hourly correlation \NumFiatGbpCorr{} and \NumFiatEurCorr{} after the
cuts), but both already sat at a discount before the cut, and the cuts changed little: the sterling dislocation rose
by \NumFiatGbpUsdtChange{} bps for USDT (\NumFiatGbpBtcChange{} for BTC), and the euro dislocation fell by
\NumFiatEurUsdtChangeAbs{} bps. The pre-recorded rule, which tested the post-cut level, classifies both as supportive; on
the more meaningful change, neither replicates the Binance.US magnitude. A plausible difference is that sterling and
euro rails closed in both directions at once and on a schedule announced in advance, whereas Binance.US opened a
one-way gate; we cannot test this.

\section{A Ledger-Failure Monitor for Web Price Feeds}
\label{sec:monitor}
A price feed or oracle built on a venue's dollar quotes inherits that venue's ledger: in July 2023, a feed reading
Binance.US BTC/USD would have understated the dollar value of every asset by
$|PD_{\mathrm{BTC}}|$, several percent, with no token at fault. The decomposition can run continuously. We evaluate a fixed hourly rule:
flag a \emph{ledger failure} when $|L|$ reaches the R1 severity threshold (100 bps) and $PD_{\mathrm{USDT}}$ agrees
with $L$ to within a quarter of its size, and a \emph{token failure} for coin $q$ when $|T_q|$ reaches the same
threshold. Periods are labelled only from documented dates.

\paragraph{Calm-period flags.} Inspecting them after the fact,
\NumMonUnresolvedHours{} of these hours fall on 7--9 May 2023, exactly the window of an unresolved venue episode that the
token-side screen had found independently (all four stablecoins at a premium against the Binance.US dollar; one
press source; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). The decomposition classifies that episode as a ledger event: USDT and BTC
moved together against the platform's dollar. The remaining \NumMonFalseHours{} flagged hours
(\NumMonCalmOutside\% of calm hours) occurred on a single day after the 2025 restoration. Splitting the calm flags this
way was done after seeing them and is disclosed as such; the cause of the May 2023 episode is undocumented. The
rail-cut detection rate is in-sample, because the rule was written after the Binance.US episode had been studied, so the
informative numbers are the absence of ledger flags in the token failures and the calm-period rate.

\paragraph{Threshold sensitivity.} These conclusions do not depend on the two settings: across all \NumSensSettings{} combinations of a 50, 100 or 200~bps threshold and an agreement
tolerance of a tenth, a quarter or a half (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}), the rule flags ledger failure in at most
\NumSensTokenLedgerMax\% of SVB or FTX hours, in \NumSensRailMin--\NumSensRailMax\% of rail-cut hours and in
\NumSensCalmMin--\NumSensCalmMax\% of calm hours, and flags USDC as a token failure in \NumSensSvbUsdcMin--\NumSensSvbUsdcMax\%
of SVB hours.

\section{What On-Chain Pools Saw}
\label{sec:onchain}
Decentralized exchanges sit outside any platform ledger, so they offer a second view. For three Curve pools whose
reproduced historical state matched on-chain quotes exactly and was validated against real swaps, we measure how much
of the stressed coin could be sold within 50~bps of par (absorption) or bought (restoration), sampled every six hours
and hourly in event windows (Table~\ref{tab:onchain}). In the token failures, absorption collapsed as pools filled
with the stressed coin (to zero for UST in May 2022 and for the USDC-to-USDT leg in March 2023), while restoration
capacity rose. In mid-June 2023, during the Binance.US withdrawal-halt window, Curve's 3pool also became USDT-heavy and
its USDT absorption fell to about a third, while USDT's token component on a venue with redeemable dollars never met the
severity rule. Pools register token flows, not ledger failures; we draw no causal link between the two June events.
Valuing counterparts at observed rather than par prices removes the event-window USDC-to-DAI absorption in March
2023 entirely, because DAI itself depegged. These measures cover three pools and no exchange depth (Appendix~\ref{app:capacity}).

\section{Token Failure: A Pre-Registered Multi-Event Study}
\label{sec:token}
The token side evaluates the decomposition on token failures selected by rule rather than by us. The registry
holds \NumRegistryEntries{} sourced entries (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}); \NumAcceptedClusters{} independent clusters
are accepted: the TerraUSD collapse (2022) and the USDC/SVB run (2023), both exploratory, and
TUSD (2024) and FDUSD (2025), both out-of-sample (Table~\ref{tab:results}).

\paragraph{The token broke; the venue's quotes held.} During SVB on Binance.US, the decomposition attributes the
dislocation to the token: over the \NumDecSvbHours{} hours of the reported window, median $D$ was \NumDecSvbD{} bps,
the ledger component \NumDecSvbL{} bps, and the token component \NumDecSvbT{} bps, against a direct Bitstamp USDC price
of \NumDecSvbDref{} bps (median ledger share \NumDecSvbShare\%). Under the plan definitions, median $|D|$ rose by
\NumAnchorDvOne{} bps relative to baseline (95\% CI [\NumAnchorDvOneLo, \NumAnchorDvOneHi]), while the within-venue
triangle wedge changed by \NumAnchorDvFour{} bps and reverted within minutes. In the out-of-sample episodes, TUSD
reached \NumTusdExtreme{} bps against USD with median $|D|$ up \NumTusdDvOne{} bps on its control (95\% CI
[\NumTusdDvOneLo, \NumTusdDvOneHi]) and an unchanged triangle (\NumTusdDvFour{} bps); FDUSD reached \NumFdusdExtreme{}
bps, median $|D|$ up \NumFdusdDvOne{} bps (95\% CI [\NumFdusdDvOneLo, \NumFdusdDvOneHi]), triangle change
\NumFdusdDvFour{} bps. UST qualifies only through a post-hoc splice amendment and is treated as exploratory
(extreme \NumUstExtreme{} bps, no recovery).

\paragraph{Negative and placebo cases.} The BUSD issuance halt (redemptions guaranteed~\cite{paxos2023busd}) fails R1
(median $|D|$ change \NumPlaceboDvOne{} bps), as do the FTX-period USDT candidate and the June 2023 USDT candidate on a
venue with redeemable dollars (peak \NumNegativeExtremeBitstamp{} bps). On Binance.US that June candidate peaks at
\NumNegativeExtreme{} bps. This is not a token failure but the start of the ledger failure in \S\ref{sec:ledger}, which is
how this study found it.

\paragraph{What the token side can and cannot say.} No accepted episode is both in-sample and confirmatory, so the
pre-registered hypothesis tests are reported as insufficient. The peg-to-wedge half-life ratio exceeds one even in the
placebo (\NumPlaceboDvFive), so it is not evidence of stress-specific persistence. Accepted episodes split into

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{For Web systems.} Price feeds and oracles that treat a quote currency as one dollar inherit both
failures. The monitor needs one unpegged asset and one reference venue per fiat currency, and in our data it separated
the two failure types with few false alarms; a deployed version would need more reference venues and a policy for
venues whose unit has no external anchor. \emph{For users,} a platform balance is a claim whose value depends on the platform's rails;
the Binance.US premium shows it can move in either direction. \emph{For measurement,} single-venue dollar prices
should not be read as token prices without the ledger check.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One strong ledger-failure case, one unexplained ledger episode, and two weak cases; the ledger side is post-search and
exploratory; the one-way-gate account is a hypothesis. Candle data use last trades with asynchronous legs; the
reference venue is a single exchange; Binance.US BTC/USD stopped trading during the impairment. Four accepted token
episodes, none confirmatory in-sample. On-chain capacity measurements (Appendix~\ref{app:capacity}) cover three Curve
pools and no exchange depth.

\section{Reproducibility}
Code, registry, frozen plan, amendment log, acquisition manifest and every report are versioned; raw files are
referenced by checksum or hash. Every number in this paper is generated by \texttt{www\_paper.macros} and
\texttt{www\_paper.rescue\_macros} from committed reports, and a fixed submission gate re-runs the tests, builds the
PDF and checks page limits. The search log, including discarded directions, is part of the artifact.

\section{Executable Capacity: Method and Limits}
\label{app:capacity}
Restoration capacity $K_t(\delta)$ is the largest quantity executable at all-in cost at most $\delta$, solved exactly on
order books and with integer StableSwap~\cite{egorov2019stableswap}, Uniswap~v2~\cite{lehar2025uniswap} and
single-range v3~\cite{adams2021uniswapv3} arithmetic. For three Curve pools whose reproduced historical state matched
on-chain quotes exactly and real swaps, on-chain absorption capacity fell in each event window, reaching zero where a
pool filled with the stressed coin, while restoration capacity rose (Table~\ref{tab:onchain}). In June 2023 absorption fell to about a third with no token failure on a redeemable-dollar
venue, so pool composition alone does not signal one. Crisis-day exchange order books are available only under paid
licences, which were declined. No exchange capacity figures are reported.
