## (b) Verdict: PARTLY NOVEL

- Not new: that oracles deviate from exchange prices (rows 1, 3, 4); that aggregators disagree and mislabel (row 2); that stablecoin and venue dollars dislocate (rows 5, 8); incident narratives (news).
- New (as far as this search shows): (i) the unit-of-account lens: scoring every served "USD" feed against a unit-corrected true-USD reference, so the error is attributed to a dislocated dollar leg (ledger dollar or stablecoin) rather than to generic noise or latency; (ii) a single audit that covers aggregators, indices and oracles side by side under the same reference; (iii) exposure accounting (hours and dollars liquidated or mispriced) tied to that unit error.
- Risk: Nadler et al. (row 1) is the nearest thing and is recent. A reviewer will ask "how is this different from oracle accuracy against a benchmark." The answer must be that their benchmark is itself exposed to the dollar problem. I could not read their full text to confirm they ignore it.
- Substantive risk (not novelty): most major oracles may be robust to a venue's ledger dollar, since Chainlink and CCCAGG-type feeds use many venues. The audit may produce a mostly-null result for oracles, with the real failures being stablecoin-quoted legs (USDT/USD, USDC/USD feed behavior in March 2023) and the aggregator front pages. This should be decided before committing, using a pilot on one episode.

## (d) Minimum viable WWW study design

Reframe from "what the web displayed" to "what machine-readable USD feeds served, scored against a unit-corrected reference." Do not claim to reconstruct web pages for 2017-19.

1. Episodes (restricted to where all arms exist): Terra/UST May 2022, FTX Nov 2022, USDC Mar 2023, Binance.US 2023. Use Bitfinex 2017-19 and the Oct 2018 USDT event only as exchange-vs-CCCAGG contrasts (no oracle arm), as a short extension.
2. Reference: the existing unit-corrected USD (docs/e2_usd_reference_design.md); define "true USD" per episode from the open-rail venue and the stablecoin-triangle correction already built in this repo.
3. Arms served to users or contracts:
   - Chainlink BTC/USD, ETH/USD, USDC/USD, USDT/USD from archive RPC (first-round dates determined empirically).
   - Pyth (2022-23 episodes) via Benchmarks API.
   - CCData CCCAGG hourly OHLCV (free key) as the aggregator arm.
   - CoinGecko hourly for the 2022-23 windows only (within the Demo 365-day limit if run soon, otherwise the paid Basic plan, which is the most likely cost: CoinGecko hourly history older than about a year needs a paid plan [V]). CoinMarketCap only if written permission or a paid plan is obtained; otherwise exclude it and say so.
4. Metrics: signed error vs reference in bps by hour; hours above 25/50/100 bps; staleness (time since last update); error attributable to the unit leg (feed's USD error explained by the stablecoin or ledger-dollar deviation) vs idiosyncratic.
5. Exposure: from Compound, Aave and Maker logs in the USDC window, recompute liquidations and repayments at feed prices vs true prices; report dollar value that would not have been liquidated (or was missed) under the corrected price. Recompute from logs; do not use news totals.
6. Pilot gate (decide in under a week): one episode (USDC Mar 2023), Chainlink USDC/USD and ETH/USD plus CCCAGG vs the reference. If the hours-above-threshold and the exposure are negligible, stop or reposition as a negative-result/robustness section of the main paper rather than a standalone WWW submission.
7. Ethics and legality: public chain data and permitted APIs only; keep API responses out of any released dataset unless the provider's terms allow (CoinGecko forbids redistribution; release derived error series, not raw feed copies); cite CoinGecko attribution; no scraping of CoinMarketCap.

Open items to resolve before any claim: first-round dates of each Chainlink proxy; CCData current free terms; whether Nadler et al.'s sample or Schwenkler et al. address a USD-denomination correction (read full texts); whether a Chainlink-specific LUNA or USDC study exists on arXiv (not found in two searches).
