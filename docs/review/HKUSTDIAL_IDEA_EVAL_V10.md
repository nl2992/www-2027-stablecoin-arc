## 1. First impression

Paper type: New Setting with a Novel Problem framing ("same name, different unit" in web value fusion), executed with a finance identification design. One-sentence story: a source's currency name can hide a different backing, one unit offset moves all that source's items, and an anchor item priced outside the source's ledger tells a unit shift from demand; on Bitfinex 2018-19 the offset's variation was about 0.97 unit shift once Tether's own discount is netted out. The story is clear in one sentence, which is a strength. The weakness is that the Web generalisation (platform credits, loyalty points, oracles) is asserted and untested; every result is a crypto exchange result.

## 3. Retrieval-grounded novelty

### 3.1 What was searched, and the limits of the search
Queries covered: truth discovery with source bias or systematic error; unit of account and USD-versus-USDT conversion in price aggregation; stablecoin-anchor and external-price netting; exchange-specific currency premia; decomposition of the Bitfinex premium and the Kimchi premium; Binance.US 2023; early warning of exchange failure from price gaps; WWW/KDD/VLDB/SIGMOD/FC/AFT papers 2020-2026 on stablecoins, oracles and exchange prices. Limits, stated so that "not found" is not read as "does not exist": the search tool does not index the ACM Digital Library, the dblp API was unreachable from this sandbox, and the web-fetch tool hit a session limit near the end, so several ScienceDirect and PDF pages were read at metadata level only. **No WWW, KDD, VLDB, SIGMOD, FC or AFT paper on stablecoin-anchor tests, exchange-specific dollars or unit-of-account bias in price fusion was retrieved.** That is "no directly overlapping work retrieved under these keywords", not proof of absence; check dl.acm.org for the 2024-2026 WWW proceedings by hand before submission.

### 3.2 Contribution 1: anchored unit test
Closest works and the axis that differs:

1. Flowerday, Gandal, Halaburda, Olson, Ardel (2026), Quantitative Finance and Economics 10(3):624-648. Same hypothesis, venue-level, BTC/USD only, no USDT price; they say the channel cannot be tested directly. Differs in identification (anchor plus external price) and in the Tether split. (Read in full in the earlier session.)
2. Borri and Shakhnov (2023), "Cryptomarket Discounts", Journal of International Money and Finance 139:102963. **Read this session** (pre-proof PDF). 135 exchanges; discounts of bitcoin prices across exchange-currency pairs, variance split into time, location and currency components, location at least half for fiat pairs; stablecoins excluded from the sample. Same family of object (an exchange-currency price wedge) but no unit-of-account claim, no anchor, no Tether, no Bitfinex episode analysis. Differs in object and mechanism.
3. Lyons and Viswanath-Natraj (2023), JIMF 131:102777. Tether's own peg deviations; the Tether leg only. Verified by search metadata.
4. Guo, Intini, Jahanshahloo (2025), "Bitcoin arbitrage and exchange default risk", Finance Research Letters 71:106364 (metadata via Cardiff ORCA and search; 16 exchanges, 2013-2024). Links gap persistence to exchange default risk. It supports the interpretation but gives no unit decomposition.
5. Choi, Lehar, Stauffer, "Bitcoin Microstructure and the Kimchi Premium" (working paper, 2018-19; SNB seminar slides found). Decomposes drivers of the Korean premium (transaction cost, confirmation time, volatility, capital controls) with FX netted at the official rate; no stablecoin anchor. Lee and Oh (2022), "The Kimchi premium and bitcoin-cashing outlets", FRL 50:103200, ties the premium to remittances to China. Seo, Koo and Yang (2024), Economic Modelling, threshold dynamics with a long-run premium near 1.24% (search summary only).
6. Not peer reviewed, metadata only: a Research Square preprint (rs-10129317, 2026, authors not captured) reports USDT's deviation from the peg as a regime signal: SVB +562 bp, FTX -231 bp cumulative abnormal premium. It confirms that "banking stress versus crypto-native stress" is already used as a USDT-price distinction, which is adjacent to the paper's 2023 account; cite only as a preprint if at all.

Verdict logic: no retrieved work nets an external token price out of an exchange's own anchor to split a venue premium into ledger and token legs; but the econometric device is standard, the verbal claim is in print, and practitioner pieces (Kruger; "Second Tether Bubble"; The Block, 12 Oct 2018) were never opened. Hence INCREMENTAL with an upside. Binance.US 2023: no academic paper retrieved; only press (CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, Blockworks, CryptoSlate, as in the earlier note).

### 3.3 Contribution 2: held-out validation
Closest: Makarov and Schoar (2020, JFE) for the common component of gaps; Borri and Shakhnov (2023) for the variance decomposition; Crépellière, Pelster and Zeisberger (2023, J. Financial Markets 64:100817; metadata via Paderborn/UZH records) for post-2018 arbitrage persistence; Hautsch, Scheuch and Voigt (Review of Finance 28(4), 2024; "Building Trust Takes Time") for settlement-latency arbitrage bounds that explain part of cross-exchange gaps. Differs only in the protocol (withheld assets, per-year and per-cell reporting, shared-dollar controls). The protocol is good hygiene and a reviewer-proof feature; it is not a novelty claim. A further caution: the score measures that alt-coin gaps move with the bitcoin gap, which within-exchange triangular arbitrage produces under both a unit shift and bitcoin-led demand. The paper says so.

### 3.4 Contribution 3: fusion benchmark and Q1
Closest: Li, Dong, Lyons, Meng, Srivastava (PVLDB 2013, "Truth finding on the deep web: is the problem solved?", stock and flight domains, from the earlier scan); Li et al. CRH (SIGMOD 2014) and the KDD 2015 evolving-truth paper (existence confirmed by search); Dong, Berti-Equille and Srivastava (2009) on dependent sources, which is the right ancestor of "sources sharing a stablecoin share an error"; the Eris/"Evaluating quality of disparate data sources: a discord-driven approach" line (arXiv 2201.13302; title confirmed, authors not) on numeric disagreement; Lazier, Thirumuruganathan and Anahideh (arXiv 2304.12573, 2023) on biased sources in truth discovery, but for categorical crowd labels. Searches for a numeric truth-discovery method with an explicit shared per-source additive offset identified across items returned none. The gap the paper claims is therefore real in the retrieved set. The "scope condition" is the robust-statistics breakdown point of the median, so the novelty is the empirical instance and the pre-registered test, not the condition.

Stablecoin-unit context added this session (all verified at abstract or page level): Watsky et al., FEDS Note 23 Feb 2024, "Primary and Secondary Markets for Stablecoins" (USDC under 90 cents in March 2023, exchanges paused one-for-one trades); Sankaewtong, Kitzler, Haslhofer, Ikeda, arXiv 2606.07442 (2026), contagion across stablecoins in the USDC depeg; Auer, Lewrick, Paulick, BIS Working Paper 1265 (2025), cross-border stablecoin flows. These support the 2023 account and the claim that exchange quote units matter, but none audits an index's USD conversion. Schwenkler, Shah and Yang, Management Science (DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2025.00611), audit vendor data; the paper cites it already, but I could not read whether it treats stablecoin quote units, so the paper should not say it does not.

## 6. The three highest-value changes (1-2 days, free data)

**E1. Independent USDT leg and extension to October 2018 (targets flaw A).** Build a Bitfinex-free decomposition: premium$_t$ = ln Bitfinex BTC/USD minus ln Coinbase BTC/USD, split as [ln Bitfinex BTC/USD minus (ln Binance BTC/USDT plus ln Kraken USDT/USD)] plus [(ln Binance BTC/USDT plus ln Kraken USDT/USD) minus ln Coinbase BTC/USD]. The first bracket is the ledger-dollar leg, the second the Tether-and-venue leg. Data in hand: Binance BTCUSDT 1-minute for 2018-09 to 2018-12 and 2019-03 to 2019-05, Kraken USDTUSD, Bitfinex and Coinbase BTCUSD (Jan-Feb 2019 Binance months are missing; a download would need the owner's yes). This covers the 15 Oct 2018 Tether depeg, which the anchored window cannot, and the 25 Apr 2019 New York Attorney General episode. Pre-state the prediction: the ledger leg carries about 0.95 or more of the variation in non-Tether weeks and a smaller share on 15 Oct 2018. If it does not, the abstract's 0.97 is a Bitfinex-USTUSD-specific result and must be narrowed. Report the Binance-versus-Coinbase gap as a placebo for "USDT-quoted bitcoin trades at the global price once Tether is netted".

**E2. Close the baseline gap on the frozen fuser (targets flaw B).** Without refitting anything, add two baselines to the existing Q1, STABLE and FUSE harnesses: (a) the median over dollar-quoted markets only, with a Bitfinex-included and a Bitfinex-excluded version; (b) a replay of the published CoinGecko rules from `INSPIRATION_SCAN.md` (4% median filter, 30-minute staleness, USDT through an on-ramp index, here approximated by Kraken USDT/USD). Add a breakdown-point curve on clean 2021 months: inject a 100 bp unit shift into k of n sources, k = 1..n-1, and plot each method's error. Pre-specify the reading: if the fuser is within noise of (a) in Q1, the abstract's last sentence changes to "matched a dollar-quoted-only rule at no loss and beat it only where a dollar-labelled venue carried a different unit". This also turns the scope condition from "one winning cell plus three events" into a curve.

**E3. Real-data size and power for the anchored test, plus a baseline-complete monitor (targets flaws A and C).** Run the same hourly anchored recipe (netted slope with the month-block interval and $J$) on every rolling six-month window from 2018-11 through 2023 using the Bitfinex USTUSD and Kraken USDTUSD series in hand, and report the share of windows in which $\lambda=1$ is and is not rejected, next to the simulated size and power. Calm windows should show an unstable $\hat\lambda$; stress windows near one. For the monitor, given the Q2 tie, tune nothing; add the baselines a reviewer will name: the Bitfinex or Kraken USDT/USD gap alarm and a rolling z-score of the BTC gap, and a label-shuffle placebo (permute label dates within year, 1,000 draws) to give an empirical p-value for 7 of 9 caught episodes. Since Q2 already shows no lead-time advantage, expect this to confirm the demotion; if the monitor cannot beat the USDT-gap alarm, demote it to one appendix paragraph as recommended in flaw C. Optional if time remains: decompose the Korean won premium with Upbit BTC/KRW, Bithumb KRW-USDT and the FRED won rate, aligned with the 2025 Economics Letters result that the Bitcoin and USDT Kimchi premia co-move at parity (title and abstract-level only; authors not confirmed), to show the anchor's FX-noise bias directly.

## 7. Verdict

**Accept with Revisions.** Lead with contribution 1 and the pre-registered contribution 3, fold 2 into 1, demote 4. Do the three experiments in order E1, E2, E3; E1 and E2 each change a headline sentence if they fail, so run them first. The pre-registered, honest-null style is the paper's strongest asset; do not trade it for a larger claim.
