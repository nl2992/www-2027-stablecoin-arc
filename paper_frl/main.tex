\title{Whose Discount? Separating Bitfinex's Dollar from Tether's}

\begin{abstract}
Bitfinex's bitcoin premium in 2018--19 was mostly a discount on its ledger dollar. Bitfinex's USDT/USD price looked like a weak test of that reading because it mixed the dollar with Tether's own discount. Netting Tether's external price, from Kraken, out of it made it track Bitfinex's bitcoin gap with slope close to one (\NumKStressDaySlopeAdj{}, up from \NumKStressDaySlopeD{}). Binance.US's 2023 offset passed the same test. Gaps shared across assets persisted in calm years, inside the fee band, and a single rate removed much of the gap on held-out assets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Bitcoin traded at a large premium on Bitfinex in 2018 and 2019. In the hours with a gap of at least 50 bps, the median gap to Bitstamp was \NumKStressMedL{} bps. The usual reading is a limit to arbitrage. Banking trouble kept dollars from reaching Bitfinex, and the gap persisted.

That reading hides a second question. What is a dollar on Bitfinex? Bitfinex quotes in USD, but a USD balance there is a claim on the exchange. It is not a bank deposit. A shortfall in that claim moves every asset quoted in it. Tether (USDT) is a second claim. It is issued by a firm linked to Bitfinex, and its own price can leave par. A gap in bitcoin could then reflect the exchange dollar, Tether, or both.

This letter separates the two. The exchange dollar moves all assets together, so gaps in bitcoin and ether share a common component. USDT is an anchor: if Bitfinex's dollar is at a discount, USDT/USD quoted on Bitfinex should move with the bitcoin gap. It did so only in part (slope \NumKStressDaySlopeD{}). The shortfall is the identification problem. Tether has its own discount outside Bitfinex. We observe it on Kraken. Netting Kraken's USDT/USD price out of Bitfinex's raised the slope to \NumKStressDaySlopeAdj{}, with a 95\% interval of \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjMoLo{} to \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjMoHi{}.

The result changes what the premium measures. Part of it priced Bitfinex's ledger dollar. Part priced Tether. The two have different remedies and different risks. A trader who sold bitcoin on Bitfinex and bought it elsewhere would have faced the first. A holder of USDT would have faced the second.

We relate to four literatures. Makarov and Schoar \citep{makarov2020trading} documented large, persistent gaps across exchanges and a component shared within a region. Griffin and Shams \citep{griffin2020untethered} linked Tether issuance on Bitfinex to bitcoin prices. Lyons and Viswanath-Natraj \citep{lyons2023keeps} studied what holds stablecoins to par. Flowerday et al. \citep{flowerday2026bitcoin} studied bitcoin on four exchanges to mid-2021 and discussed a discount on Bitfinex's dollar as one reading of the premium. They state that they do not decompose it. We do, and we test the split against an external price. Our contribution is the identification, not a new estimator.

\section{Data and design}
We use public candles for bitcoin and ether on Bitfinex, Coinbase and Bitstamp from 2017 to 2023, and hourly candles for nine further assets on Bitfinex and Coinbase. USDT/USD prices come from Bitfinex, Kraken and Binance.US. Binance.US candles cover 2022 to 2025. Bitstamp is the open-rail reference, and all hourly prices are medians of traded-minute closes.

Let $L$ be the Bitfinex--Bitstamp bitcoin gap, in basis points of log price. Let $D$ be $10^4$ times the log of Bitfinex's USDT/USD price. If the gap in $L$ is a pure shift in Bitfinex's dollar, USDT moves with it: $D=L$, and the slope of $D$ on $L$ is one. If crypto demand lifts bitcoin but leaves USDT alone, the slope is zero. If Tether has its own discount $X$ that does not depend on Bitfinex, then $D=L+X$ plus noise. Let $X$ be $10^4$ times the log of Kraken's USDT/USD price. Then the slope of $D-X$ on $L$ is the clean test of the ledger-dollar shift. The residual $T=D-L$ is the part of USDT's move that the bitcoin gap does not explain.

We estimate slopes by OLS on daily medians. Intervals are block bootstraps over weeks and months (1,000 draws). As a robustness check we instrument $L$ with the Bitfinex--Coinbase ether gap, which shares the ledger dollar but not the bitcoin quote error. Kraken's USDT market traded in \NumKCovSharePct\% of the hours of the stress window (\NumKCovTraded{} of \NumKCovHours{}), and the longest untraded run was \NumKCovLongestGap{} hour. Kraken's own bitcoin price stayed close to Bitstamp's, with a median absolute gap of \NumKStressKrakenBtcGapMedAbs{} bps in the stress window. We therefore take its USDT price as outside Bitfinex's ledger.

The 95\% interval was \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjMoLo{} to \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjMoHi{} over months and \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjWkLo{} to \NumKStressDaySlopeAdjWkHi{} over weeks. Bitfinex's bitcoin premium was therefore mostly a discount on the exchange dollar. Tether's own discount entered only the anchor, which is why the raw slope fell short of one.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
The premium on Bitfinex was not one thing. In the stress window, the median bitcoin gap was \NumKStressMedL{} bps. The USDT quote on Bitfinex moved by a median of \NumUAnchorStressD{} bps. Tether's own price on Kraken was at \NumKStressMedX{} bps. A reader who tracked only USDT/USD on Bitfinex would have attributed too little of the gap to the exchange dollar. A reader who ignored USDT would have attributed all of it to bitcoin.

For Makarov and Schoar \citep{makarov2020trading}, the gap is a limit to arbitrage and a regional component. Our result adds that on Bitfinex the arbitrage constraint was a claim on the exchange, and that the claim can be tested with a stablecoin whose price is observed elsewhere. Against Griffin and Shams \citep{griffin2020untethered}, we do not test manipulation; we show that Tether's price on Bitfinex carried both a ledger-dollar and a token component. For Lyons and Viswanath-Natraj \citep{lyons2023keeps}, the Kraken price is the external token price. Flowerday et al. \citep{flowerday2026bitcoin} raised the ledger-dollar reading but did not decompose the premium; Table~\ref{tab:anchor-ext} does.

\paragraph{Limitations.} First, Kraken's USDT price is a proxy for Tether's external value, and Kraken has its own rails and fees. The residual $T-X$ was \NumKStressMedTmX{} bps in the stress window, and it was larger in some calm years. Second, USDT began trading on Bitfinex on 2018-11-27, so we have no anchor for the earlier banking episodes. Third, the 2018 slope of $D$ on $L$ is above one (Table~\ref{tab:anchor}) on a short sample, and we do not interpret it. Fourth, the placebo has few large-gap hours. Fifth, the held-out score was high in 2017--2019 and lower in calm years, when the gap is small and asset-specific noise is a larger share of it. Sixth, fee schedules rest on secondary sources. The decomposition is statistical, and it does not show what caused the discount on Bitfinex's dollar.

\section{Conclusion}
Bitfinex's 2018--19 bitcoin premium was mostly a discount on its ledger dollar; Tether's own discount contaminated only the stablecoin anchor. Netting Tether's external price out of Bitfinex's USDT price made it track the bitcoin gap with slope close to one (\NumKStressDaySlopeAdj{}). Binance.US in 2023 shifted only the ledger dollar (slope \NumUAnchorBnusSlope{}). Price comparisons across exchanges should say which dollar and which stablecoin they use.
